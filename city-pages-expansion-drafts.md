## TROY — /troy-sewer-cleanup

### Why Troy Homes Are at Higher Risk

Troy's neighborhoods span a wide range of housing ages, from 1950s–60s split-levels near Big Beaver Road to sprawling estates in areas like Northfield Hills. Many homes from this era still run on original clay tile or cast-iron sewer laterals — materials that develop interior scale and brittle joints over decades, giving tree roots an easy entry point. Troy's mature, tree-lined subdivisions compound this: root systems actively seek out the moisture inside older sewer lines. Combined with Michigan's freeze-thaw cycles shifting soil around buried pipes, and the high water table near the Big Beaver corridor, Troy homes are especially prone to backups during spring thaw and heavy summer storms.

**Common triggers we see in Troy:**
- Backups following spring thaw as frozen ground releases and shifts around aging pipe joints
- Storm surges near the Big Beaver Road corridor overwhelming aging storm drainage
- Root intrusion in older clay laterals throughout established subdivisions
- Sump pump failures during high-water-table conditions common to lower-lying lots

### Troy-Specific FAQs (add to existing FAQ accordion)

**Does Troy's storm drainage system contribute to sewer backup risk?**
Troy's Streets and Drains Division maintains an extensive storm water drainage network regulated under the Oakland County Storm Water Permit. In older sections of the city, heavy rain events can still overwhelm capacity faster than newer subdivisions built to more recent drainage standards, increasing backup risk in established neighborhoods.

**What does sewage cleanup typically cost in Troy, MI?**
Costs depend on how much water intruded and how long it sat before extraction. Minor, quickly-addressed backups can run in the low hundreds; significant Category 3 events requiring structural drying and material removal can run into the thousands. We provide an on-site itemized estimate before any work begins.

**How long does structural drying take after a Troy basement backup?**
Typically 3 to 5 days with industrial dehumidification, depending on how long the water was standing and how much of the structure absorbed moisture.

## BIRMINGHAM — /birmingham-sewer-cleanup

### Why Birmingham Homes Are at Higher Risk

Birmingham's housing stock includes a significant share of homes from the late 19th and early 20th century, concentrated in historic districts and neighborhoods like Poppleton Park and the areas around Quarton Lake. These older homes commonly carry original cast-iron or galvanized plumbing, and many sit on aging clay sewer laterals installed decades before modern PVC became standard. Birmingham's mature tree canopy — a defining feature of its historic neighborhoods — means root intrusion is a frequent contributor to backups, and the city has periodically undertaken sewer lining and improvement projects specifically to address aging infrastructure in its oldest sections.

**Common triggers we see in Birmingham:**
- Backups in historic-district homes tied to original clay or cast-iron laterals
- Root intrusion from mature trees along Birmingham's older residential streets
- Heavy spring and fall rain events overwhelming aging storm and sanitary lines
- Basement flooding near Quarton Lake and other low-lying residential sections

## BERKLEY — /berkley-sewer-cleanup

### Why Berkley Homes Are at Higher Risk

Berkley's housing stock is largely bungalow-style construction from the 1940s and 1950s, built with original clay or cast-iron sewer laterals that are now reaching the end of their expected service life. Berkley sits within the Rouge River watershed, and its relatively flat topography means heavy rain has limited places to drain quickly. Combined with an older municipal sewer system that in parts of the city still handles both storm and sanitary flow together, heavy rain events can push more water into the system than it's designed to carry — forcing it back up through basement floor drains in older homes.

**Common triggers we see in Berkley:**
- Backups tied to Berkley's older combined storm/sanitary sewer sections during heavy rain
- Root intrusion in clay sewer laterals common to 1940s–50s bungalow construction
- Sump pump overload during multi-inch downpours typical of the Rouge River watershed area
- Basement flooding affecting original hardwood floors and plaster walls in older homes

During heavy rain, this can push more volume through the system than it's designed for, increasing the chance of backups reaching residential basements — particularly in the city's older neighborhoods.
