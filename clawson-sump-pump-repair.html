Common Sump Pump Failure Modes We Diagnose in Clawson

A sump pump rarely fails with advance warning. In Oakland County, failures typically occur during sudden 2-inch cloudbursts or rapid winter thaws when water tables surge. When our technicians arrive on-site in Clawson, we systematically diagnose the following common mechanical and electrical breakdowns:

🔴 Float Switch Hang-Up & Burnout

Over 60% of all sump emergencies in Clawson trace back to the float switch. As water fills the basin, mechanical vibrations can twist the pump, causing the float ball or vertical slider to pin against the rough wall of the basin crock. The pump either runs dry until the motor burns out, or never activates at all, causing a rapid overflow.

🔴 Check Valve Failure & Water Cycling

The inline check valve prevents discharged water in the vertical PVC pipe from draining back into the pit once the motor shuts off. When spring-loaded or flapper valves crack from age, gallons of expelled water fall back into the pit immediately, forcing the pump to cycle continuously every 15 seconds until it overheats.

🔴 Discharge Line Freezing & Air-Locks

During Michigan's brutal freeze-and-thaw cycles, exterior discharge lines running to the lawn or ditch freeze solid. Without an auxiliary exterior relief valve, water has nowhere to escape, creating immense backpressure that locks the impeller. In warm weather, sediment and small gravel from weeping tiles can also clog intake screens.

🔴 Severe Grid Outages (DTE Surges)

Summer storms that bring the heaviest rainfall often bring down high-voltage utility lines across Clawson. If your home relies exclusively on a standard 120V AC plug-in pump, the moment your neighborhood loses power, your basement is entirely defenseless against rising groundwater.

Frequently Asked Questions

What causes a sump pump to fail during a heavy storm?
The most common causes of sump pump failure are localized power outages, stuck float switches, mechanical motor burnouts, and debris clogging the intake screen. Having a secondary backup pump is highly recommended.
Should I install a battery backup sump pump system?
Yes, absolutely. Power grids often fail during heavy rainstorms. A battery backup system runs automatically when your primary pump loses power or is overwhelmed, preventing catastrophic basement flooding.
How long do commercial-grade sump pumps last?
Most residential-grade sump pumps last between 5 to 7 years. We install premium, commercial-grade submersibles with cast-iron housings that offer superior longevity and higher flow rates during storm surges.
