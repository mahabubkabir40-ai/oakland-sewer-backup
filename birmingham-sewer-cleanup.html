Why Birmingham homes are at higher risk

Birmingham's housing stock includes a significant share of homes from the 1920s through the 1950s, in neighborhoods like Poppleton Park and the areas around Quarton Lake. These older homes commonly carry original cast-iron plumbing and sit on clay sewer laterals installed decades before modern PVC became standard. Birmingham's mature tree canopy means root intrusion is a frequent contributor to backups, especially in low-lying sections near Quarton Lake.

What to do in the first 10 minutes of a sewer backup in Birmingham

  1. Stop using water. Do not run faucets, flush toilets, or run the washer or dishwasher.
  2. Keep people and pets out of the water. Sewage carries bacteria and other pathogens.
  3. Do not plunge or snake the drain. A household snake can push dirty water into the subfloor.
  4. Leave the basement if water is near outlets, the panel, or the furnace. Shut power off only from a dry location.

Birmingham questions

Are older Birmingham houses more prone to sewer backups?
Houses in Birmingham's older sections often still have clay or cast-iron laterals. Those materials crack, separate, and invite roots, which is why backups show up more often there than in newer construction.
