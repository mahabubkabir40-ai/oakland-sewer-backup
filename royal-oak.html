Royal Oak sewer main, lateral, and basement water help

Royal Oak's Sewer Division maintains about 300 miles of public sewer and publishes the basement-water number. For the cleanup inside the house, a local cleanup crew handles the visit, and they'll tell you when they can be there.

Call (248) 825-8312

Royal Oak's sewer main: which pipe is the city's

The City of Royal Oak Sewer Division says it maintains about 300 miles of sanitary and storm sewers. For basement water, the city lists (248) 246-3300 on weekdays from 7:30 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. After hours, the police non-emergency number (248) 246-3500 dispatches sewer personnel. The city is responsible for the main. The homeowner is responsible for the lateral up to and including the connection to that main.

Call (248) 246-3300 when the city should look at the main, especially while water is still coming in. Call (248) 825-8312 for a local crew to pump or dry the house. While you wait, stop using water, keep people and pets out, and leave the basement if water is near the furnace.

Twelve Towns, then the George W. Kuhn basin

Royal Oak's flood-response page says the city is a member of the former Twelve Towns program, now the George W. Kuhn Retention Treatment Basin, and that the basin was expanded in 2006. The regional storage for this system is the George W. Kuhn Retention Treatment Basin, under the I-75 overpass at 12 Mile Road in Madison Heights, which can hold and treat 150 million gallons. In a heavy downpour, Royal Oak says street flooding is usually caused by debris sitting on the sewer grate, so clearing leaves and grass clippings off the grate in front of your house helps. Flooded streets during working hours (Monday–Friday, 7:30 a.m.–2:30 p.m.) go to the Department of Public Service at (248) 246-3300; the city says street water usually isn't handled after hours unless it is excessive. The basin's size and how much of its wet-weather flow is stormwater are on the George W. Kuhn Drainage District page. Membership does not tell you whether the street main or your lateral failed today.

August 11, 2014, using the city's gauge and the federal declaration

FEMA-4195-DR, declared September 25, 2014, covered Macomb, Oakland, and Wayne counties for individual and public assistance. A National Weather Service paper describes about 4 to 6.5 inches in parts of those counties, most of it in about four hours on August 11. Oakland County 115 reported the next day that Royal Oak's DPS rain gauge recorded 4.98 inches that day, 1.12 inches of it in 30 minutes. No count of damaged houses is stated here.

Older blocks, and which problem you have

Royal Oak's residential blocks run off Main and Washington, along Woodward, and toward West Ten Mile.

If sewage came through a floor drain, a basement toilet, or a laundry standpipe, open sewer backup cleanup in Royal Oak. The pumping step, once you have hired someone, is sewage extraction in Royal Oak. Storm water, a window well, or a sump, with the drains quiet, is flooded basement cleanup in Royal Oak. Drying and the decision about finishes are water damage restoration in Royal Oak. The pit itself is sump pump repair in Royal Oak. Cleaning after sewage has been removed is basement sanitization in Royal Oak.

Insurance paperwork and the 45-day letter

Photograph the drain before anything is thrown away. Ask your insurer about a sewer-backup endorsement. The crew sets the scope and the price, and you confirm that company's license and insurance. A claim against the city is not the same call as (248) 825-8312. The 45-day written notice is in the sewer backup claim guide. The basement flood checklist already lists (248) 246-3300 for the next storm.

Neighboring cities use different sewer rules

Berkley publishes a single combined pipe. Troy splits flow across three districts. Birmingham's system is gravity with no city pump stations. Clawson's after-hours line is Troy dispatch, not Royal Oak police. Open the overview for the city where the house stands: Berkley, Troy, Birmingham, and Clawson.

Royal Oak questions

Which number does Royal Oak use for the city sewer?
(248) 246-3300 on weekdays, 7:30 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. After hours, the police non-emergency line (248) 246-3500 dispatches sewer personnel. The city checks the main in the street. (Source: City of Royal Oak, Responding to Street and Basement Flooding)
Is the pipe from my Royal Oak house to the street mine?
Yes, up to and including the connection to the main. The city is responsible for the main. A cleanup company should not guess which pipe failed. A plumber's camera inspection checks a specific lateral. (Source: City of Royal Oak Sewer Division)
Did the August 2014 federal disaster declaration include Oakland County?
Yes. FEMA-4195-DR was declared September 25, 2014, for Macomb, Oakland, and Wayne counties. Oakland County 115 reported the next day that Royal Oak's DPS gauge recorded 4.98 inches on August 11, with 1.12 inches in 30 minutes. The reports do not give a count of damaged homes. (Source: Oakland County 115, August 12, 2014) (Source: Federal Register, FEMA-4195-DR)
What cleanup do I need if the Royal Oak floor drain never moved?
Open flooded basement cleanup when the water is from a storm, a window well, or a sump. Open water damage restoration if finishes are already soaked. Open sewer backup cleanup only when sewage came up through a drain, a basement toilet, or a laundry standpipe.
Does the George W. Kuhn basin keep my Royal Oak basement dry?
Not by itself. Royal Oak's flood-response page says the city is in the former Twelve Towns program, now the George W. Kuhn Retention Treatment Basin, expanded in 2006. That does not tell you whether your lateral or the street main failed. If you believe the city is involved, send written notice within 45 days of discovery. (Source: City of Royal Oak, Responding to Street and Basement Flooding) (Source: Michigan Legislature, MCL 691.1419)
